One breath

Half a litre of warm, wet air

10,000,000,000,000,000,000,000

molecules leave you with every exhale.

You do it about 20,000 times a day, and so does everyone around you.

0.5 L of air at 20 °C ≈ 0.02 mol ≈ 1.2 × 10²² molecules · 12–20 breaths a minute

A single breath is about half a litre. That is roughly ten thousand billion billion molecules. Write it out and it is a 1 followed by 22 zeros. Each one bumps into neighbours billions of times a second. Most people assume all of that just blends into the wind and disappears. Keep that assumption in mind; we are going to test it.
