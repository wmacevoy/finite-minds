The limit

About two weeks

7 days + 3½ + 1¾ + … = 14 days

Measure every swirl of air perfectly and you still cannot forecast past this. After it, any small difference has spread across the whole planet.

Lorenz (1969) · Palmer (2024), Physics Today · Zhang et al. (2019): 14–15 days for midlatitude weather

Here is why better instruments do not save us. Say today’s measurements give a week of good forecasts. Measure twice as finely and you add smaller swirls, which go wrong twice as fast, so you gain only half a week. Do it again and you gain a quarter of a week. Add it all up and even perfect measurements give you about fourteen days. Modern weather-model experiments agree: about two weeks.
