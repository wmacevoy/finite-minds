How weather forgets

Weather forgets where it started

1963

Small differences grow

Edward Lorenz restarted a weather simulation from numbers rounded to three decimals. The result soon looked nothing like the original.

1969

There is a hard limit

In real air the smallest swirls amplify errors fastest. Better measurements barely help: the horizon stays at about two weeks.

Lorenz (1963), J. Atmos. Sci. 20:130 · Lorenz (1969), Tellus 21:289

The famous butterfly effect comes from Lorenz’s 1963 paper. He rounded a number and the simulated weather went somewhere completely different. The deeper result is his 1969 paper. Real air has swirls inside swirls inside swirls, and the small ones change fastest. That means there is a limit you cannot buy your way past. Perfect instruments would not let you forecast more than about two weeks ahead.
